How the model writes drums, one 16th note at a time

At every step the trained LSTM reads the previous step and its position in the bar, gives a chance to all 512 drum combinations, and one is drawn at random according to those chances. The pick is written into the loop and fed back as the next input. In the constrained systems, combinations whose kick or snare does not match the skeleton are blocked first. These are real runs of the trained model (training seed ).

Temperature reshapes the model's chances before the random pick.

0.8 · cooler: likely options get even likelier. Safer and more repetitive.

1.0 · as learned: the model's own chances, unchanged. All experiments use 1.0: the results table, the follow-up analyses and the listening clips.

1.2 · warmer: chances are flattened, so unlikely options come up more often. More surprising; too warm drifts into chaos.

0.8 and 1.2 appear only here and in three qualitative clips.

Inside the model at this step

The detailed view is available at temperature 1.0 (the one the experiments use).

Input · 25 numbersThe previous step's 9 drums (1 = hit) and the position in the bar (a single 1 among 16).
LSTM layers · output h and memory cEach square is one of the layer's 256 units. Output h (top, −1 blue to +1 orange) is what the layer passes on. Memory c (bottom) is its internal notebook: gates decide what to erase, write and read out at each step. Its values grow much larger (log colour scale, up to ±128).
Output · 512 chancesOne per drum combination, darker = likelier (log scale). In constrained mode, crossed-out squares are blocked: their kick or snare does not match the skeleton.
Pick · back to the inputOne combination is drawn by chance (orange outline). Its 9 drums become the input of the next step, so the model always continues from its own choices.
